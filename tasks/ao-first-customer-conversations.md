# The first 15 customer conversations, and the outreach that follows

**Prefix:** `a-` — high priority, actionable now. No code. This is the item
everything else on the marketing list depends on, and the easiest to skip.

## Why this is a task card

Every other card here is a guess about what buyers want, written by people who
are not buyers. Fifteen conversations turn the guesses into facts, and they
cost nothing but nerve. If the price, the export list, or the comparison
design is wrong, this is where you find out — before building, not after.

## Who to talk to

You already have test users. Start there. Then, in order:

1. **Building code consultants**, small firms in the GTA. They do
   alternative-solutions and existing-building work, so they need the code as
   it read on a permit date. They bill hourly.
2. **Construction and litigation lawyers, and expert witnesses.** Highest
   pain. "What did the code say in 1999" has a dollar value attached.
3. **Municipal plans examiners and property-standards officers.** Slow to buy,
   but they renew.

Fifteen is the target. Ten is enough to see a pattern.

## The four questions

Ask these. Do not pitch. A conversation where you talk is a conversation that
teaches you nothing.

1. **What did you use before this?** Names the real competitor, which is
   usually a filing cabinet, a colleague, or a paid subscription we do not
   know about.
2. **When did you last need an old edition, and what did it cost you?** Turns
   the problem into a number.
3. **Would you pay for this yourself, or would the firm?** Decides whether the
   price is a personal one or a firm one. This decides the price.
4. **What would make this untrustworthy for you?** The most valuable question.
   The answer is the roadmap for the verification work.

Write down the words they use. Those words become the site copy. Do not
paraphrase into our own vocabulary.

## Outreach, after the first five conversations

Send 20 personal emails a week. Not a newsletter.

- One sentence about their actual work. Look it up first.
- One line about the 1997 edition, and that it is not on e-Laws.
- One link — a live search result, or a chapter of the demo video
  (`tasks/b-demo-video-and-cpd-talk.md`), for a provision they care about.

A deep link into a real result is the demo. Do not attach a deck.

Sources for names: the Ontario Building Officials Association member list, the
Ontario Association of Architects directory, and the firm pages of code
consultancies. All public.

## What to do with the answers

- Update the pricing decision (`tasks/b-price-change-to-299.md`).
- Cut or reorder `tasks/b-provision-exports.md` if one export is named
  repeatedly and another never comes up.
- Feed the vocabulary into the landing page and the proof article.

## Done when

- 15 conversations are done and the notes are written down in one place.
- The four answers are summarised, with the exact phrases people used.
- The price decision is made *from* those notes, not before them.
- 20 outreach emails a week are going out.
